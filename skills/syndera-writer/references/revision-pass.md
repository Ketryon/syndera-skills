# Revision pass

Read this once a draft exists, not before you write. It describes patterns that make text read as machine-written. They are signs to look at, not rules to apply mechanically: what the brand's published posts do always wins, including on punctuation, emoji and length. If the brand's founder writes with dashes and lists of three, so does the post.

Why these patterns happen: a model tends to write the statistically safe version of a sentence. Each pattern below is that safe version showing through. Fixing one usually means saying the specific thing instead, not swapping a word.

## Structure

- **Staged contrasts.** "It's not just X, it's Y", "X isn't about A, it's about B". Usually a way of sounding insightful without making a claim. Say Y.
- **The reflexive three.** Lists of exactly three adjectives or clauses, again and again. Use as many items as there really are.
- **The trailing summary.** A final clause or line restating what the post just said ("…making it easier than ever to stay on top of things"). Cut it, or replace it with the one concrete detail it was gesturing at.
- **Identical rhythm.** Every sentence the same length and shape. Vary it the way the brand's own posts do.
- **The announced hook.** A first line that describes the post rather than starting it ("Here's what we learned", "Let's talk about X"). Start with the thing itself.

## Inflation

- **Inflated significance.** "Game-changing", "revolutionary", "a testament to", "plays a pivotal role". If the thing matters, the specific fact shows it.
- **Vague attribution.** "Experts say", "many people find", "studies show" with no source. Name the source or drop the claim; never invent one.
- **Feelings on the reader's behalf.** "You'll love…", "We're thrilled to…" in a brand that doesn't talk that way.

## Formatting

- **Decoration as structure.** Emoji used as bullet points, bold on every other phrase, headers in a 200-character post. Match the brand's formatting.
- **Hashtag stacks.** A block of tags appended to the end that the brand's own posts don't use.
- **Generic sign-offs.** "What do you think? Let us know below!" when the brand never asks.

## How to use this

1. Read the draft once against the list above.
2. For each pattern you find, ask whether the brand's own posts do the same. If they do, leave it.
3. Otherwise rewrite toward the specific claim, not toward a synonym. Replacing one stock phrase with another isn't a fix.
4. Stop after one pass. Chasing every pattern makes text stiff in a different way.
